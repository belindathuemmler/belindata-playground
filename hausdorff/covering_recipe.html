Hausdorff’s Recipe, Step by Step

The object is a curve in the plane. A cover is a family of pieces \(U_1, U_2, \dots, U_N\) that together hide it; here every piece is a disc of diameter \(\delta\). Each piece is priced \((\operatorname{diam} U_i)^s\) and the prices are added up; here \(s = 1\), so every piece costs its diameter, like a length. Shrink \(\delta\), and the pieces must follow the curve ever more closely. Try another cover of the same curve: the sum changes a little, and Hausdorff takes the cheapest of them all.
