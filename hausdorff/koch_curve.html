Weighing the Koch Curve

Start with a segment of length 1 and build the curve step by step. Every step turns each piece into 4 pieces of a third of the length. Then measure the result in three different dimensions.

Measured in 1D (length)
\(4^n \cdot (1/3)^n\) → \(\infty\)
Measured in 2D
\(4^n \cdot (1/3)^{2n}\) → \(0\)
Measured in dimension 1.26
\(4^n \cdot (1/3)^{nd} = 1\) for every \(n\)

Each box adds up \((\text{piece length})^s\) over all pieces, the same sum Hausdorff’s recipe uses. Length explodes, area vanishes, and only at \(d = \log 4/\log 3\) does the number stay put.
