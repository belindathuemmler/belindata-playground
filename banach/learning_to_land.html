Learning to Land

A toy lander, small enough to apply the rule \(T\) to every guess at once. Altitude \(1\) to \(6\), falling speed \(0\) to \(3\), two actions: burn (one unit slower, costs \(1\)) or coast (one unit faster, free). Touching down at speed \(0\) or \(1\) pays \(+100\), any faster is a crash, \(-100\); \(\gamma = 0.8\). The guesses start as random numbers, the way a network does. Each round replaces every guess by its target, \(Q \leftarrow TQ\). Watch the lander fly the current best actions, and watch two numbers: the gap between guess and target, which is all the lab can see, and the distance to \(Q^*\), which only the theorem can promise is shrinking.
